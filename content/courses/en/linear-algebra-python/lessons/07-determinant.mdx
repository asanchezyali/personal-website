For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}$ the determinant is defined as

$$
\det A = \det\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = ad - bc
$$

The formula admits a geometric interpretation that makes it unnecessary as an object of
memorisation.

## Geometric interpretation

Let $Q = [0,1]^2$ be the unit square, of area $1$, generated by $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$.
Its image $T_A(Q)$ is the parallelogram with sides $(a,c)$ and $(b,d)$, and its area is

$$
\operatorname{area}(T_A(Q)) = |\det A|
$$

The determinant is therefore the **signed area scale factor**: for any measurable region
$S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ the relation
$\operatorname{area}(T_A(S)) = |\det A| \cdot \operatorname{area}(S)$ holds. The absolute value is
necessary, since $\det A$ may be negative while an area may not.

## The sign

The sign of $\det A$ records **orientation**. If $\det A > 0$, the transformation preserves the sense
of rotation of the plane; if $\det A < 0$, it reverses it, and $T_A$ includes a reflection.

For $A = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1)$ the result is $\det A = -1$: area is preserved in magnitude, but
the half-plane lying to the left of $\hat{\imath}$ comes to lie on its right.

## Vanishing of the determinant

$$
\det A = 0 \iff \operatorname{rank}(A) < 2 \iff \text{the columns are linearly dependent} \iff A \text{ is singular}
$$

The four conditions are equivalent and have already appeared in earlier lessons from different
angles: the degenerate span of the lesson on linear combinations, the non-invertibility of the lesson on composition and inverses, and the rank criterion of
the lesson on linear systems are manifestations of a single fact. Geometrically, $T_A(Q)$ collapses to a segment and its
area vanishes.

```python
import numpy as np

np.linalg.det(np.array([[2, 1], [1, 1]]))   # 1.0  → non-singular
np.linalg.det(np.array([[2, 4], [1, 2]]))   # 0.0  → singular
```

In floating-point arithmetic the test $\det A = 0$ is not reliable. Deciding singularity numerically
relies on the condition number or on `np.linalg.matrix_rank`, which uses the singular value
decomposition with an explicit tolerance.

## Origin of the formula

The parallelogram with sides $(a,c)$ and $(b,d)$ is inscribed in the rectangle of dimensions
$(a+b) \times (c+d)$. Subtracting from that rectangle the two triangles and the two surplus
rectangles yields $ad - bc$. The expression $ad - bc$ **is** the signed area, not a computational
rule independent of it.

## Multiplicativity

$$
\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)
$$

The property is immediate from the geometric interpretation: if $T_B$ multiplies area by $\det B$ and
$T_A$ by $\det A$, the composition multiplies it by the product. The direct algebraic proof is
considerably more laborious.

```python
A = np.array([[2, 0], [0, 1]])
B = np.array([[1, 1], [0, 1]])

np.linalg.det(A @ B)                  # 2.0
np.linalg.det(A) * np.linalg.det(B)   # 2.0
```

As a corollary, if $A$ is invertible then $\det(A^{-1}) = 1/\det(A)$, which requires
$\det A \neq 0$ and recovers the singularity criterion.
