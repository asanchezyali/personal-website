The determinant is usually introduced as a formula to be memorised, $ad - bc$. It is, however, a
number with a precise geometric meaning, and once that meaning is understood the formula no longer
requires memorisation.

## What the determinant measures

The determinant of a matrix is **the factor by which area is multiplied** when the transformation is
applied.

Consider the unit square, the one formed by $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$, of area 1. On
applying the matrix, the area of the resulting figure *coincides* with the determinant.

The purple region is the image of the unit square, and the numerical value accompanies it
throughout:

- $\det = 1$ indicates that area remains constant. Rotations and shears belong to this group.
- $\det = 3$ indicates that area triples.
- $\det = 0.5$ indicates that area halves.

## The meaning of the value zero

When the determinant reaches zero, the purple region disappears: the entire plane has flattened onto
a line.

That condition connects the previous lessons in a single statement: **$\det A = 0$ if and only if
the columns of $A$ are linearly dependent**, if and only if the span degenerates, if and only if $A$
lacks an inverse. These are four equivalent formulations of one and the same fact.

```python
import numpy as np

np.linalg.det(np.array([[2, 1], [1, 1]]))   # 1.0  → invertible
np.linalg.det(np.array([[2, 4], [1, 2]]))   # 0.0  → singular
```

## The sign

The formula does not suggest it, but the determinant can take **negative** values.

Setting $a = -1$ and leaving the remaining entries at the identity, the determinant becomes $-1$.
The area retains its magnitude, but the plane has been **flipped**: what lay to the left of
$\hat{\imath}$ now lies to its right.

The sign therefore records orientation. If positive, the plane preserves its sense; if negative, a
reflection has taken place.

## Why the formula takes that form

With the geometric interpretation established, the expression $ad - bc$ ceases to be arbitrary.

The columns of the matrix are the two sides of the parallelogram into which the unit square has been
transformed: $(a, c)$ and $(b, d)$. The area of a parallelogram defined by those sides is exactly
$|ad - bc|$, a result obtained by subtracting the surplus triangles from the rectangle that contains
it. The formula *is* the area.

## An immediate property

$$\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)$$

Expressed in words, the property is almost self-evident: if $B$ triples area and $A$ doubles it,
applying both in sequence multiplies it by six. The algebraic proof is laborious, whereas the
geometric justification is immediate.

```python
A = np.array([[2, 0], [0, 1]])
B = np.array([[1, 1], [0, 1]])

np.linalg.det(A @ B)                          # 2.0
np.linalg.det(A) * np.linalg.det(B)           # 2.0
```
